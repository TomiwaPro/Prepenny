# Voice

Who we write for: someone tired at 11pm after a night shift, on a cheap phone. Calm, specific, never blaming.

## Rules

- British English.
- The name is PrePenny: two capital Ps, one word.
- Short sentences. Give the number instead of describing it: "£4.53 left until Friday", never "gain clarity over your money".
- Dates as "Fri 30 Oct", adding the year only when it isn't this year.
- Money as £4.53, with a proper minus sign (−£30.00).
- Say "short" for a shortfall.
- One warning at a time, with the exact amount and date.
- Error messages say what to do: "Type an amount, for example 25."
- Use: Coming in, Going out, Left after this, Paid off on, Lowest point ahead, Tight, Short, Not ticked yet, Update balance, Move to next payday.
- Never use in the app: transaction, recurrence, projection, accrual, categorisation.
- No exclamation marks. No emoji.
- No long dashes (— or –) anywhere people read. Use a full stop, comma or colon. Write ranges as "2 to 3".
- Colour is never the only signal. A status always has a word: Tight, Short, Paid off.
- All text lives in one strings file so the app can be translated later.
- Every screen and sheet starts with one plain sentence saying what it's for, so a 16-year-old understands it straight away: "Your bank shows money that's gone. This shows money that's going to go, and what you'll have left each time." (D213)
- Every control says in words what it does: "Switch account", "Show another month", "Got it". A choice asks or says what's being chosen, with an answer for each option in words: "What happened?" with "I worked extra hours" and "I missed a shift". (D213)

## Banned

Banned in the app, website, emails and store listings:

seamless, effortless, empower, unlock, elevate, journey, game-changer, revolutionise, supercharge, cutting-edge, leverage, robust, harness, delve, hassle-free, stress-free, all-in-one, next-level, "smart" as praise, simply, "take control of your finances", "financial freedom", "peace of mind", "we've got you covered", "say goodbye to", "in today's world", "whether you're ... or ...", "it's not just ..., it's ...", "AI-powered".

Also avoid:

- three adjectives in a row ("simple, smart and secure")
- questions as headlines ("Tired of money stress?")
- promises nobody can check.

## Website and store listings

- Real screenshots of the app showing the example plan. No stock photos, no drawings of people, no made-up reviews or statistics.
- Use the tagline "Plan every penny before payday." where the brief says (the website, the app store listings and the first setup screen).
- The "Why I made this" note is written by the owner. Never write it; put [OWNER'S NOTE] in its place.

## Before finishing

Before finishing any task that adds or changes text, search for every banned word and for — and –, and fix them.

These rules apply to text people read, not to names inside the code.
