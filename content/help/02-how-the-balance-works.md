# Every payment shows what you'll have left after it

Your bank shows money that's gone. PrePenny shows money that's going to go, and what you'll have left each time.

## Where the numbers start

PrePenny starts from the balance you typed in, on the day you checked it. Then it takes off each payment going out and adds each one coming in, in date order. On the same day, money coming in counts first, then moves between your accounts, then money going out.

Every payment on **Coming up** has what's left after it under its amount, such as "£4.53 left".

## The lowest point ahead

**Lowest point ahead** on Home is the lowest your balance will be at the end of any day, looking 12 months ahead, with the payment that takes it there. It's the one number to watch.

## Tight and Short

- **Tight** means your balance will be under the amount you chose in **Warn me when it goes under**. You can change it in **More**, then **Accounts**.
- **Short** means you'd run out of money: below £0.00, or below your overdraft limit if you have one. Home says how much and when, such as "£40.00 short on Sun 1 Nov."

A word always goes with the colour, so you never have to tell them apart by colour.

## Left to spend

**Left to spend until** your next payday is the spending money you planned up to then, such as food and travel, less what you've recorded with **I spent**. If spending all of it would take your balance under the amount you want to keep, a line under it says how much to keep it to, such as "Spend no more than £16.54 to keep at least £20 until Fri 30 Oct."

## Not ticked yet

A payment from before today that isn't ticked is still counted, under **Not ticked yet** on Coming up. Tap it to mark it as paid once it has gone, or to change it.

## About now, and what the bank showed

Once money has moved since you last checked your bank, Home's big figure says "About" what's in your account now, with what your bank showed under it. To start from your bank's real figure again, use **Update balance**.
