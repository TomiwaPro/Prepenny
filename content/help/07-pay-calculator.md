# The pay calculator works out what lands in your bank

From your hours and pay rates, it works out your take-home pay after tax, National Insurance, pension, student loan and anything else that comes off. It's an estimate, not tax advice.

## Where to find it

In PrePenny, go to **More**, then **Pay calculator**. Anyone can use it on the website too, at [prepenny.co.uk/pay-calculator](https://prepenny.co.uk/pay-calculator/), without an account. It works it all out on your phone: nothing you type is sent anywhere.

## What it asks

- Your hours and rate for each kind of hours, and any fixed amount, such as a shift premium.
- Your tax code, from your payslip. Most people have 1257L.
- How often you're paid, and when.
- A workplace pension, a student loan plan, and anything else that comes off, if you have them.

For exact tax, type the figures from your last payslip: its tax week or month, your taxable pay so far this tax year and the tax paid so far. Without them, the result is an estimate.

It uses the rates for the tax year from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027, from GOV.UK.

## Putting it in your plan

In the app, one tap puts the result in your plan as that payday's pay, marked as an estimate. When the real pay lands, change it to the real amount.

You can save your hours as a usual week, so you can start from it next time. **Overtime or missed shift** on Home uses it too.

## Checking a payslip

**Check my payslip** compares the figures on a payslip with what PrePenny works out, and lists any that are different. If something looks wrong, ask your payroll team.
