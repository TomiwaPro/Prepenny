## Waiting for the owner

One can't: on Sun 8 Nov, Left to spend runs to Thu 12 Nov, the day before payday, and Sam has no spending money due then, so it's £0.00, not £30.00. The point of that check still holds: the £80.00 left on the pay-later card isn't in Left to spend. The test now checks it's the same as the plan without the food budget. Say if £0.00 is right, or if you meant a different day.
2. **A missed shift and the shift premium (D123)** (before Phase 2, which builds the I missed a shift sheet). "I missed a shift" takes the shift's hours × rate off the payday, but leaves the shift premium as it is, because Sam's premium isn't the same for every shift (£114.69 and £142.29 in his 3-shift weeks, £210.23 and £172.01 in his 4-shift weeks). The proposal: keep it that way, and let the sheet take off an amount of premium as well if the person types one. Say if a missed shift should take off part of the premium on its own.
3. **Shifts paid every 2 weeks or monthly (D125)** (before Phase 2). A shift is paid on the first payday on or after the start of its working week moved on by the weeks pay is behind. For weekly pay like Sam's that's exact. For Dee, paid every 2 weeks one week behind, a shift in the week of Mon 2 Nov counts from Mon 9 Nov, so it's on her first payday on or after that. Say if pay every 2 weeks or monthly should work differently, for example by pay period.
4. **A pension taken after tax (D126)** (before Phase 2, which builds the pay calculator screen). The percentage typed in is what comes off the pay, as the payslip shows it. With "relief at source", the pension company claims the tax relief itself, so it's not added to take-home pay. Say if the calculator should instead ask for the pension's full percentage and work out what comes off the pay.
