## Left for later phases

Found by the step 1g engine review. None needs an answer from the owner, but each must be done in the phase named.

1. **Adding and changing records as edits** (first step of Phase 2). The engine's edit commands change payments, ticks, balances, debts' statements, spends and pay adjustments, but there's no command to add or change a payment, account, debt, budget, savings goal, pay profile or pay week. Setup, Add a payment and the other Add sheets need them, so every change goes through the same checks and can be undone (brief 5.4). The usual hours for "I did overtime" after D136 need one too.
2. **The app keeps out what the engine accepts** (Phase 2): only currencies with hundredths (D98), a budget's start date of today or later, like a payment's "Next payment" (D86), and a balance date of today unless the person changes it.
3. **A budget that starts after the plan's last day** (Phase 2) shows no current period, as if it had ended. The budget screen should say when it starts.
4. **Ticks and changes after a series' dates move** (Phase 3). Undoing an old edit after "this and all later ones" has moved that series' dates by a whole week or month can put a tick back on a different payment. Storing each tick's and change's payment number, with the Phase 3 records, lets undo check it.
5. **A partner's view of a payment to a private debt** (Phase 3). A payment from the joint account to Jess's private debt makes the engine refuse Tom's Debts screen, because the debt isn't in his records. The server's copy for Tom should leave that link out (D65).
6. **Small pay cases** (Phase 2, with the pay calculator screen): a payday moved back into the tax week of the payslip just before it is refused, and on week 1/month 1 the year so far skips the weeks in between when the payslip is an older one.
7. **The credit card comparison when payments are due the day after the statement** (Phase 2): in a short month both fall on one day and the comparison pays one minimum twice. Rare in the UK.

## Waiting for the owner

Questions that need an answer before the step that uses them. Move each one to `docs/DECISIONS.md` once it's answered.

1. **Monthly pay that covers work in the month before (D131)** (before Phase 2, which builds the I did overtime sheet). For monthly pay, the last day covered is in the payday's own month: pay on the last working day covering work up to the 15th covers up to the 15th of that month, as in your test. That doesn't fit pay that covers work in the month before, such as pay on the 5th for work up to the last day of last month: the engine would read it as the last day of the month the payday is in. The proposal: setup works out from the date typed in, "Fri 5 Feb pays for work up to Sun 31 Jan", whether the last day covered is in the payday's month or the month before, and the engine stores that with the day of the month. Say if that's right, or if monthly pay should always cover work in its own month.
2. **Left to spend when the plan stops before payday (D28, D112)** (before Phase 2, which builds Home). Left to spend says "until" the next payday, but it only counts the days the plan looks at. Ali's next payday after Mon 11 Jan is Mon 26 Apr, more than the 3 months the Plan screen shows by default, so the same line says £455.00 with a 3-month plan and £525.00 with a longer one. The proposal: Left to spend, and the "Spend no more than" line, always look as far as the day before the next payday, however far the plan screen looks. Say if that's right.
3. **When the main pay stops (D14, D84)** (before Phase 2, which builds the Plan screen). When the main pay has no payday left in the plan, for example after Ali's last loan payment in April, or a job that has ended, the plan shows one long period from the last payday to the end, and there's no Left to spend. The proposal: after the last main payday, the plan is grouped by calendar month, and Left to spend runs to the end of this month. Say if that's right.
4. **"Last working day" and a changed bank holiday list (D78)** (before Phase 3, when the bank holiday list starts being refreshed on phones). For pay on the last working day, the date a tick or one-off change is stored against is the last working day itself (D78). If the bank holiday list changes, for example when GOV.UK adds a one-off holiday or a plan moves to another nation, that date can move, and a tick stops matching: the payday then shows as not ticked and is counted again. The proposal: store these against the month's last day, and move to the working day before as the weekend and bank holiday rule, so ticks always stay with their payday. The dates people see don't change. Say if that's right.
5. **The credit card comparison on payment day (D102)** (before Phase 2, which builds the comparison). The comparison of the minimum, a fixed amount and the full balance starts from Left now, but leaves out a payment due today, so on payment day, before it's ticked, it counts a month too many: £25 a month shows 22 payments instead of 21. The proposal: count a payment due today when the card's payment for today isn't ticked yet. Say if that's right.
6. **Roll-over on a pay-later card (D110, D111)** (before Phase 2, which builds the budget sheet). With roll-over on, the bill for a period that has ended is still estimated at the budget until its real amount is typed in, while the unspent part has already rolled into the next period. So £50 not spent of £200 is planned to leave twice: in this bill (£200) and the next (£250). The plan shows £50 less than the truth until the real bill is typed in. The proposal: with roll-over on, a bill for a period that has ended is estimated at what was spent, so the unspent part counts once, in the next period. Say if that's right, or if the safer double count should stay.
